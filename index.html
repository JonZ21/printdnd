01 The gap

A finished print
is a stopped printer.

The machine is fine. The filament is fine. The queue is full. It stops because getting a part off a plate is a job for hands, and hands go home.

Whatever that gap turns out to be — an hour, a night, a long weekend — it is dead time on a machine that was ready to keep going.

This is the whole problem we started on. Not printing faster. Just not waiting.

05 Why it matters

The bed is the first thing.
It isn't the last.

Print farms already solve throughput by buying more printers. What nobody has taken out is the minute of human attention every machine needs between jobs — clearing the last part, reloading a spool, starting the next file. Take that out and how hard a printer runs stops depending on who's awake.

Running today

  • A BracketBot working alongside a Bambu Lab X1 Carbon
  • Teleoperated bed lifts, recorded and logged
  • The episode being replayed on this page

Where it goes

  • The reset running without a person driving it
  • Swapping filament when a spool runs out
  • Queueing work across several machines
  • A print setup that doesn't stop when the room empties

The right-hand column is what we're building toward, not what we're selling. Items move left when they actually work.
